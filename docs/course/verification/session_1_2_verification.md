# Verification Record — Session 1.2: Mission Need & Stakeholder Analysis

## Verification Date: 2026-05-04

### 1. NASA SEH Process 1: Stakeholder Expectations Definition
**Claim:** Process 1 covers eliciting stakeholder needs, defining ConOps, use cases, and Measures of Effectiveness (MoEs).

**Verification:** CONFIRMED. NASA SEH §4.1 describes Stakeholder Expectations Definition as the process of identifying stakeholders, eliciting their expectations, defining the concept of operations, and establishing MoEs. The outputs include stakeholder requirements and a baseline ConOps.

**Source:** NASA SEH §4.1; NPR 7123.1D §3.2.1
**Confidence:** HIGH

### 2. SMART Requirements Framework
**Claim:** SMART = Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Traceable.

**Verification:** CONFIRMED. While NASA SEH Appendix C uses a different checklist structure (shall statements, single requirement per statement, performance-based, verifiable, etc.), the SMART acronym is widely used in the SE community. NASA SEH Appendix C §C.2 lists characteristics of well-written requirements that align with SMART principles. ECSS-E-ST-10-06C §5.2 also requires requirements to be verifiable and traceable.

**Caveat:** "SMART" is not a NASA-coined acronym — it originates from project management (Doran, 1981). NASA's own checklist in Appendix C is more detailed. Course should reference both.

**Source:** NASA SEH Appendix C; ECSS-E-ST-10-06C §5.2
**Confidence:** HIGH (framework), MEDIUM (attribution)

### 3. MoE/MoP/TPM Hierarchy
**Claim:** MoE (Measures of Effectiveness) measure how well the system satisfies stakeholder needs; MoP (Measures of Performance) measure technical characteristics; TPMs (Technical Performance Measures) track key parameters through development.

**Verification:** CONFIRMED.
- MoE: Operational measures from user perspective (NASA SEH §4.1.4)
- MoP: Technical measures of how well the product performs (NASA SEH §4.2.4)
- TPM: Parameters tracked over time to assess design maturity (NASA SEH §6.7.3)

**Source:** NASA SEH §4.1.4, §4.2.4, §6.7.3
**Confidence:** HIGH

### 4. Problem Statement Structure
**Claim:** A good problem statement identifies: what the problem is, who is affected, what the impact is, and what constraints exist.

**Verification:** CONFIRMED as best practice. NASA SEH §4.1.1 discusses the need to "clearly define the problem before solving it" and recommends understanding the operational need, the gap between current capability and desired capability, and constraints on the solution space.

**Source:** NASA SEH §4.1.1
**Confidence:** HIGH

### 5. Stakeholder Categories
**Claim:** Stakeholders include: end users, operators, sponsors/funders, regulatory bodies, launch providers, ground station operators, data consumers, general public (for government missions).

**Verification:** CONFIRMED. NASA SEH §4.1.2 identifies stakeholder categories including customers, users, operators, regulatory authorities, and other affected parties. The specific list varies by mission but the categories above are representative.

**Source:** NASA SEH §4.1.2
**Confidence:** HIGH
